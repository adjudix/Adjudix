# SIAC (Singapore International Arbitration Centre) — Competitor Analysis

Source: https://siac.org.sg/ (fetched Sept 2026 via live browser with real screenshots — WebFetch/automated fetch was blocked with HTTP 403 Cloudflare protection on every URL tried; a real Chrome session accessed it without issue, confirming the block targets bot-pattern requests, not browsers). Pages visited: Home, SIAC Gateway, SIAC Academy, Fee Calculator, SIAC Panel of Arbitrators, Services, plus the Arbitration mega-menu.

## Overview
SIAC is a top-3 global arbitral institution (with ICC and LCIA), independent and not-for-profit, founded 1991, headquartered at Maxwell Chambers, Singapore. Operates under the SIAC Rules (7th Edition, 2025) plus SIAC Investment Rules, a SIAC-SIMC Arbitration-Mediation-Arbitration Protocol, and UNCITRAL Rules administration. This is the highest-tier reference site in the whole set and, once actually browsed live, turns out to be executed with real design and product discipline — genuinely the aspirational quality bar for Adjudix.

## Primary Purpose
Institutional arbitration administration at global scale, combined with a fully productized digital layer (SIAC Gateway for e-filing/case management) and a sustained, multi-year training operation (SIAC Academy) — i.e., all three of Adjudix's core pillars (arbitration, ODR, training) exist here simultaneously and are each individually excellent.

## Component Inventory
- **Full-bleed cinematic photography hero** — actual Singapore skyline/Marina Bay Sands at sunset, with a translucent blue-to-teal gradient band overlaid at the bottom third carrying the headline in white ("Where the World Λrbitrates" — note the wordmark's distinctive triangular "Λ" arch motif deliberately substituted for the letter A, echoing the logo) and two pill-shaped ghost-outline CTA buttons with animated arrow icons ("Learn More," "For Enquiries")
- **Consistent secondary-page header pattern**: every interior page (Fee Calculator, Panel of Arbitrators, Services) reuses a different but consistently-styled full-bleed photo band — notably a real photo of **Maxwell Chambers Suites** (SIAC's actual building) rather than generic stock imagery — with the page title overlaid, and a **left-rail contextual mini-nav card** (dark blue, white text, thin divider lines) mirroring whichever top-nav dropdown the visitor came from
- **Icon-labeled 3-card row** on the homepage (Arbitrators / Fees / Practice Notes — circular blue-outline line icons: scales, coin, document-and-pencil) alongside two plain text-CTA cards (Rules / Model Clauses) — a mixed-density card pattern, not uniform
- **Real interactive Fee Calculator** — four real inputs: (1) Amount in Dispute in SGD, (2) Sole Arbitrator vs Panel of Three, (3) Party Information (Singapore vs Overseas — this affects the filing fee, a nuance MCIA's calculator lacks), (4) a Yes/No toggle for "Case Conducted under Streamlined Procedure" — plus a helpful inline link to a third-party currency converter (oanda.com) for foreign-currency users. This is the most sophisticated fee calculator of all institutions studied.
- **Searchable/filterable Panel of Arbitrators directory** — genuinely live, with 5 filter fields (Title/Salutation, Name search, Nationality/Geographical Origin, Language(s), Primary Practice Location), a "Search" button, a "Reset" button, and a results table (Title / First Name / Last Name / Nationality / Language / Practice Location / **Profile** link) — this is a real, functioning searchable people-directory, the single best example of this component pattern across the entire competitor set (better than ICCA's plain contact table, far better than ICA India's static PDF-by-profession lists)
- **News/highlights carousel** with dated MoU-signing announcements styled as a blue-to-teal gradient card row (SIAC actively signs cooperation MoUs with other institutions worldwide — e.g., Getulio Vargas Foundation in Brazil, Nanjing Arbitration Commission, Bhutan ADR Centre — a visible "expanding global network" signal)
- **"SIAC Notices" sidebar-plus-cards module** — a persistent left-column utility block (operating hours, public-holiday closure dates, a call-ahead-before-visiting notice) sitting beside the main Rules/Model-Clauses cards — practical, physical-office-aware content alongside the digital content
- **Events calendar with dated badge cards** (month/day badge + time + title + "Get Tickets" pricing when applicable) and **Featured/Upcoming tab toggle** plus "Subscribe to calendar" — genuinely calendar-app-like event browsing, not a flat list
- **Multi-year course archive pattern** (SIAC Academy) — every year from 2021–2026 kept as its own visible, browsable table (date, programme name, register link), with named recurring course series (e.g., "Enforcement of Arbitral Awards: Theory & Practice" — a single series run across 10+ jurisdictions over multiple years; "Specialist Arbitration Series" covering Construction, Energy/Oil & Gas, Maritime/Shipping, IP/Tech/Crypto, Insolvency/Banking/Finance) — training content is treated as a permanent, cumulative content library, not ephemeral event listings
- **Role-based portal landing page (SIAC Gateway)**: rather than one generic "login" link, the Gateway's own hero presents **four distinct pill buttons for four different audiences** — E-Filing, Parties Portal, Arbitrators Portal, Help — each presumably routing to a different authenticated experience
- **404 page** styled consistently with the rest of the site (full nav retained, centered message, functioning search box, full footer with social icons including YouTube, LinkedIn, Spotify — SIAC runs a podcast — and WeChat, signaling deliberate China-market reach)

## Training Components
**SIAC Academy** is exceptionally mature — confirmed via a real multi-year course archive (2021 through 2026 already scheduled):
- Governed by a named **Academy Audit Panel** drawn from the SIAC Board of Directors and Court of Arbitration (named individuals with credentials shown, e.g. Lucy Reed as Chair, Claudia Annacker, Lawrence Boo, Bernard Hanotiau) — curriculum oversight is personally attributable to known senior figures, not an anonymous committee
- Course types span: rules-mechanism deep-dives (Emergency Arbitration, Expedited Procedure, Early Dismissal), an annual "Review of Key Arbitration Developments and Decisions," "Arbitration 101" foundational courses, an "Investment Arbitration 101" primer, practice-based practicums (Discovery and Document Production), a long-running **Specialist Arbitration Series** by industry (Construction, Energy/Oil & Gas, Maritime/Shipping, IP/Tech/Crypto, Insolvency/Banking/Finance, Cryptocurrency Disputes as a 2026 addition), a multi-year **"Enforcement of Arbitral Awards: Theory & Practice"** series covering one jurisdiction per session (Singapore, China, India, UAE, USA, Brazil, South Korea, Indonesia, Philippines, UK completed across 2021–2025), regional "Academy" editions (SIAC India Academy, SIAC China Academy, SIAC Middle East Academy, SIAC South East Asia Academy, SIAC Russia Academy), and an annual flagship **SIAC Academy Annual Lecture**
- Delivery is explicitly **online by default**, with select sessions hybrid/in-person — the reverse default from CIArb's separate-SKU approach, but the same underlying idea of format being a deliberate, stated choice per course
- This is unambiguously the deepest, most sustained training operation studied, ahead even of CIArb's qualifications catalogue in year-over-year content richness (though CIArb's is more tied to formal membership-grade progression)

## Membership Components
No public tiered membership ladder for general professionals (confirmed absent, consistent with the earlier assessment). **YSIAC (Young SIAC)** functions as SIAC's community/pipeline layer, parallel to MCIA's Young MCIA — a first-level nav item, not buried. The **SIAC Panel of Arbitrators** functions as the credentialing/roster layer instead of a membership ladder — practitioners are on the panel (searchable, profiled) rather than holding a "membership grade."

## Arbitration Components
- **Multiple named protocols beyond core Rules**, each solving a specific real-world need: **Arb-Med-Arb (AMA) Protocol** (combines mediation and arbitration in one structured product — resembles the "Med-Arb" hybrid model explicitly named in Adjudix's own handwritten notes' services sketch), **Restructuring and Insolvency Arbitration (RIA) Protocol** (a dispute-type-specific protocol, same pattern as ICA India's construction Dispute Board service and Adjudix's own "Specialized Panel for specific disputes" idea)
- **Ad Hoc Appointment Services** — SIAC will appoint arbitrators even for arbitrations it isn't administering under its own rules — a distinct, monetizable service line separate from full case administration
- **Fundholding** — SIAC holds/manages the parties' deposited funds for ad hoc arbitrations as a neutral escrow-like service — a specific, named trust-building service worth being aware of
- **Authentication/Certification** — a named service for authenticating/certifying documents related to arbitration, a small but real administrative revenue line
- Real interactive Fee Calculator with jurisdiction-sensitive (Singapore vs Overseas party) and procedure-sensitive (Streamlined vs standard) inputs — more sophisticated than MCIA's or ICA India's

## ODR Components
**SIAC Gateway is fully confirmed live and is the richest ODR reference in this entire set.** The Gateway has its own distinctly-branded landing page (separate visual identity: "SIAC GATEWAY" wordmark using the same triangular-arch logo motif, over a different full-bleed Singapore skyline photo) with **four explicit, separate portal entry points**: E-Filing, Parties Portal, Arbitrators Portal, and Help — a genuinely role-based front door rather than one generic login. Supporting content includes dedicated "How-To Guides" (Guide for Arbitrators, Guide for Claimants and Respondents, Guide for Electronic Filing — audience-specific onboarding documentation), its own Terms & Conditions, its own Contact Us, and its own FAQ set (confirmed distinct questions: what is Gateway, how to access it, who to contact, whether software download is required, when it's accessible). This role-segmented portal structure is the single most directly reusable pattern for Adjudix's own ODR platform IA.

## Design Language
Now confirmed directly (previously unconfirmed): a clean, restrained, corporate-premium palette built on **navy/deep blue as the primary brand color**, a **teal/turquoise gradient as a secondary accent** used specifically for hero overlay bands and highlight/news cards, white space used generously, and a small red accent stroke used only inside the logotype itself (the horizontal bar over the "Λ"/A). Typography is a clean, modern, geometric sans-serif with confident but not oversized headlines. Photography is real and specific (actual Singapore skyline, the actual Maxwell Chambers Suites building) rather than generic legal stock imagery — this alone sets it apart from MCIA and ICA India. Buttons are consistently pill-shaped with a small animated arrow glyph. Icons are simple blue-outline circular line icons (scales, coin, document). Cards use thin colored underline accents beneath headings rather than heavy borders or shadows. Overall impression: restrained, confident, "quietly expensive" — closer in spirit to a premium global professional-services brand (think: a Big Four consultancy or a top-tier law firm's marketing site) than to a typical government/legal-institution template. This is the clearest evidence in the whole competitor set of what "SIAC-level visual quality" actually concretely looks like, and it validates that Adjudix's brief (aiming for this tier) is asking for a real, achievable, describable standard — not a vague aspiration.

## Strengths
- Exceptionally deep, multi-year, personally-governed training operation (SIAC Academy) with named oversight figures and a real course archive
- SIAC Gateway is a fully realized, role-segmented ODR product with its own sub-brand, dedicated guides, FAQs, and four audience-specific portals
- Panel of Arbitrators is a genuinely functional, filterable, profiled directory — best-in-class among all sites studied
- Named protocols for specific needs (Arb-Med-Arb, Restructuring/Insolvency) turn "we handle hybrid/niche disputes" into concrete, marketable, individually-paged products rather than a vague capability claim
- Fee Calculator handles real-world nuance (jurisdiction of party, procedure type) beyond a simple amount-in/fee-out tool
- Consistent, restrained, premium visual identity executed carefully across every page type (homepage, tool pages, directory pages, 404 page)
- Real building/place photography (Maxwell Chambers) grounds an otherwise very digital-feeling brand in physical legitimacy
- Active, visible global-expansion signal via a running feed of institutional MoU announcements
- Deliberate reach into non-Western channels (WeChat presence, SIAC India Academy, SIAC China Academy, regional academies) shows genuine internationalization beyond an English-only, Singapore-only posture

## Weaknesses
- Sheer breadth of the Arbitration mega-menu (11 items) and overall site depth could overwhelm a first-time visitor without excellent search/wayfinding (a real search icon is present in the nav, mitigating this somewhat)
- Multiple overlapping "getting a neutral" paths (Panel search, Ad Hoc Appointment Services, Gateway Arbitrators Portal) could benefit from a single clearer entry decision point for a confused first-time user
- Training content, while deep, is presented as long chronological tables rather than a filterable catalogue (no visible filter by topic/format/level on the Academy page itself)

## Elements Worth Reusing (for Adjudix)
- **Role-based portal landing page** for the ODR platform (separate, clearly labeled entry points for "I'm filing," "I'm a party in an existing case," "I'm a panelist/arbitrator," "I need help") — directly modeled on SIAC Gateway's four-button pattern, and a strong concrete answer to Adjudix's own "ADR-i Portal for Dispute Resolution" notes
- **Multi-input, context-aware fee calculator** (amount, arbitrator count, party jurisdiction/domestic-vs-international, and a procedure-type toggle) rather than a bare 2-field version — directly serves Adjudix's own "Rules & Fees Calculator" note and its "Domestic/International" dispute-type distinction
- **A real, filterable, profiled panel directory** (filter by specialization/jurisdiction/language, results link to individual profiles) for Adjudix's "Our Professional Panel" pages — the clearest best-practice reference in the whole set
- **Named, productized protocols for specific scenarios** (an Adjudix equivalent could name and page its own "Arb-Med Protocol," or a "Construction Dispute Protocol") rather than leaving these as generic panel categories
- **Multi-year, cumulative training archive** presented as a permanent browsable record (not just "upcoming events") — supports Adjudix's Training Institute credibility over time as it accumulates course history
- **Named curriculum-oversight body** (an Adjudix "Academy/Training Board" of a few named senior figures) to lend the Training Institute the same personal-credibility signal SIAC Academy has
- **Consistent secondary-page header pattern** (full-bleed real photography + left-rail contextual mini-nav) as the standard template for every interior page — efficient to build once and reuse everywhere
- **Distinctive, brand-specific logotype detail** (SIAC's Λ-arch substitution) — Adjudix should consider a similarly small, deliberate wordmark device rather than a purely generic text logo

## Elements Worth Improving (things Adjudix should do better)
- Provide a single, clear "which path is right for me" decision point up front, since even SIAC's excellent IA has some redundancy between panel search, ad hoc appointment, and Gateway portals — Adjudix can differentiate by being simpler and more guided for a first-time, less arbitration-savvy visitor (a real advantage of being newer and smaller)
- Make the training catalogue filterable by topic/format/level rather than only chronological, once Adjudix's own course volume grows past a handful of entries
- Since SIAC's marketing site was Cloudflare-blocked to automated tools (a real, observed limitation), Adjudix should keep its own site crawlable/indexable by AI assistants and research tools where it doesn't compromise security — increasingly relevant for discoverability as more research and referrals happen via AI tools rather than direct browsing
