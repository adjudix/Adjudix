# Sama (sama.live) — Competitor Analysis

Source: https://www.sama.live/ (fetched Sept 2026 via live browser, screenshots captured)

## Overview
Sama (legal entity: ODRWays Solutions Private Limited, founded 2019, Bangalore) is a venture-backed, consumer-facing Online Dispute Resolution platform — featured on Shark Tank India. It is the single most important design/product reference in this entire set for Adjudix's **ODR Platform** module, precisely because it is not a traditional legal institution at all: it's a tech startup that happens to operate in the arbitration/mediation space. Scale claims: 1 crore+ (10M+) disputes filed, 11 lakh+ (1.1M+) resolved, 14-day average resolution time, a panel of 3,000+ neutrals across 500+ districts in India.

## Primary Purpose
Enable businesses (B2B, e.g. banks, e-commerce platforms like Snapdeal) and individuals to resolve disputes entirely online via Online Mediation, Online Arbitration, and Online Lok Adalat, backed by a large neutrals panel and a tech-forward case-management stack (OCR document intake, dashboards, transcription).

## Navigation Structure
Minimal top nav, audience-segmented rather than content-segmented: **For Businesses / For Individuals / Become a Resolutionary** (arbitrator/mediator recruitment) + language selector. This is a fundamentally different IA philosophy than every legal institution studied — Sama organizes by *who you are*, not by *what service you need*.

## Component Inventory
- **Full pastel-lilac hero** with a warm documentary-style B&W photo (real people, not stock-corporate), a Hindi-English bilingual tagline ("Suljhao, Magar Pyaar Se!" — "Resolve it, but with love!"), a solid dark-plum rounded CTA button ("Get in Touch"), and a "As Seen On Shark Tank India" badge — startup/consumer branding, not legal-institutional branding
- **Animated stat-counter row** ("Crunch the Numbers!" — 1cr+ / 11L+ / 14 Days, values appear to tick/update) — a strong, simple trust component
- **Split feature-story blocks** pairing a claim + a real product screenshot: "Save Time" next to a phone-mockup with an actual line chart ("Disputes Filed" by year); "Lower Legal Costs" next to a browser-chrome mockup showing a "Savings Analysis" dashboard with a bar chart — i.e., **product screenshots embedded in device frames** as the primary proof mechanism, not text claims alone
- **Named case studies with hard numbers** (Snapdeal: 50%+ resolved within 30 days via Online Conciliation; unnamed large private bank: 90% legal-cost savings via "Sama Med-Arb Clause") — a proprietary named contract-clause product ("Sama Med-Arb Clause") is itself a component/asset worth having
- **Feature-card grid ("Platform Features")**: OCR-based Evidence Review, Document-led Case Filing, Detailed Insights/Dashboards, Transcription Support — each a colored card (alternating light and dark-plum backgrounds) with a short benefit statement, some paired with a small UI screenshot inset (e.g., "Upload Documents" step-1/step-2 flow preview)
- **Trust/legitimacy strip**: "Award Upheld in Saket Court, New Delhi" as its own callout — proactively citing a specific court case that validated enforceability; "Secure & Trusted Platform" (ISO 27001, India-hosted servers, encryption) as a distinct trust block
- **Partner-logo strip** grouped by category: Platforms / Courts / Government Bodies (not just a flat logo wall — segmented by relationship type)
- **Structured lead-gen contact form** with a "Type of Query" dropdown that pre-routes intent: Business Enquiry / Government Department Enquiry / Join as Arbitrator-Mediator-Case Manager / Careers / File a Dispute / "I received a notice from Sama" / Other — plus a City of Residence field (Bangalore / Outside Bangalore) — one form serving many different visitor intents via branching
- **Individual-consultation product**: a paid (₹2,500) 60-minute expert consultation as a lead-qualification step before formal filing — a clever low-commitment ODR entry point for individuals unsure if their matter even qualifies
- **Testimonial carousel** mixing professional roles (Supreme Court lawyer, retired District Court judge, law-firm lawyer) with some testimonials given in Hindi/Devanagari script — deliberate bilingual authenticity, not translated-to-English-only
- **Neutrals-recruitment funnel as its own 2-step visual component**: Step 1 (take a 15-hour compliance-certified course) → Step 2 (begin paid case work) — a clear, short pipeline diagram

## Training Components
Sama's only "training" is the **15-hour self-paced ODR certification course**, explicitly compliance-anchored to **NITI Aayog and ICODR (International Council for Online Dispute Resolution) guidelines** — i.e., it borrows external regulatory/standards credibility rather than inventing its own competence framework. Course completion is the sole gate to paid work on the platform (Case Management, Mediation, or Arbitration roles) — training exists purely as a funnel into the supply side of the marketplace, not as a standalone education product for its own sake. This is a much thinner training model than CIArb's, but a very clean, single-purpose one worth studying for Adjudix's own arbitrator/mediator-onboarding pipeline specifically (as distinct from its broader professional-training ambitions).

## Membership Components
No consumer "membership" concept at all. The closest analogs are (a) the neutrals panel (supply-side, via the course-then-case-work funnel) and (b) implicitly, business clients who integrate Sama's dispute clause into their own contracts (the "Sama Med-Arb Clause"). Not a useful model for Adjudix's membership-ladder design, but validates that **panel-empanelment can be run as its own lightweight funnel** separate from any broader membership program.

## Arbitration Components
- Names three specific mechanisms it enables: **Online Mediation, Online Arbitration, Online Lok Adalat** — notably includes Lok Adalat (an India-specific statutory conciliation forum), which Adjudix's own handwritten notes also explicitly reference ("Lok Adalat" appears in the services sketch) — Sama is direct evidence this is a real, marketable mechanism to include alongside arbitration/mediation/conciliation
- Proprietary named contract clause product (**Sama Med-Arb Clause**) — pre-drafted clause businesses insert into their own contracts to route disputes to Sama; directly maps onto Adjudix's own "Arbitration Clause Drafting" and "Model Clauses" notes — worth productizing as a named, brandable asset rather than a generic downloadable template
- Publishes **Redacted Awards** as a public resource (transparency into actual case outcomes, anonymized) — a distinctive trust/transparency device none of the other sites in this set do
- **Empanelment Norms** and a **Feedback Form for Neutrals** exist as standalone published pages — i.e., panel governance and quality-control mechanisms are made visible, not just internal process

## ODR Components
This is the core of Sama's entire product and the richest reference material in the whole competitor set:
- **OCR-based Evidence Review** — computer-vision-assisted document-type classification on upload, explicitly marketed as removing manual document-sorting burden
- **Document-led Case Filing** — filing initiated by uploading documents rather than filling long structured forms first (documents-first UX, not form-first)
- **Dashboards** — real analytics screenshots shown include a "Disputes Filed" time-series line chart and a "Savings Analysis" bar chart with a "View full report" action, framed inside literal browser-chrome and phone-frame mockups — signals dashboards serve both case-progress tracking AND cost/ROI reporting for business clients, not just case status
- **Transcription Support** — call recordings auto-transcribed via LLMs, "delivered reliably at scale"
- **ODR Login** as a distinct top-level nav/footer item — a real client portal exists and is a first-class product entry point, not buried
- **Security/compliance framing**: ISO 27001 compliance, India-based hosting, encryption — stated explicitly and prominently, appropriate given it's a platform handling sensitive dispute documents

## Forms
- Multi-intent contact/enquiry form with a "Type of Query" dropdown (7 distinct intents) and a City-of-Residence field — the most sophisticated lead-routing form of any site studied
- Individual consultation booking (paid, ₹2,500)
- Neutral/arbitrator course signup
- "I received a notice from Sama" — a specific respondent-side intake path (someone being filed against, not filing themselves) — a UX case easy to overlook but essential for any real ODR platform (Adjudix will need an equivalent "I've been named in a dispute" entry point, not just a "file a case" one)
- Feedback Form for Neutrals (post-engagement quality feedback from panel members)

## Dashboards
Confirmed real: case-progress/analytics dashboards for business clients (charts, "View full report" actions), shown via actual product screenshots rather than described abstractly. This is the strongest direct visual evidence in the entire competitor set of what a genuinely good ODR dashboard component should look like (clean card-based chart layout, dark sidebar icon nav, in-app browser chrome for context).

## Design Language
Modern consumer-tech/startup aesthetic, sharply distinct from every legal-institution site studied: soft pastel-lilac and off-white backgrounds, a single confident dark-plum/aubergine accent used for primary CTAs and dark feature cards, warm documentary-style black-and-white photography of real people (not stock "handshake" imagery), generous whitespace, rounded corners throughout (buttons, card containers, device-mockup frames), a friendly geometric sans-serif typeface, and bilingual (Hindi/English) copy woven naturally into headlines and testimonials rather than segregated to a language switcher alone. Product screenshots are shown *as* screenshots (inside literal phone/browser frames) rather than illustrated abstractly — a "show, don't tell" proof pattern. Overall tone is warm, approachable, and consumer-friendly ("Suljhao, Magar Pyaar Se") rather than austere/authoritative — a deliberate contrast to the "trust through gravitas" approach of MCIA/SIAC/CIArb.

## Strengths
- Only site in the set with real, embedded product-dashboard screenshots — genuinely proves the tech works rather than just claiming it
- Audience-first IA (For Businesses / For Individuals / Become a Resolutionary) is simpler and more action-oriented than the service-first mega-menus of CIArb/SIAC
- Multi-intent branching contact form routes many different visitor types through one component efficiently
- Named case studies with specific, credible numbers (50% resolved in 30 days; 90% legal-cost savings) rather than vague claims
- Publishes redacted awards and empanelment norms — real transparency into outcomes and panel governance
- Bilingual, warm brand voice broadens accessibility/relatability beyond an English-only legal-elite audience
- Low-commitment individual entry point (₹2,500 consultation) reduces friction for first-time/unsophisticated users unsure if ODR is right for them
- Explicitly proactive on enforceability trust ("Award Upheld in Saket Court") — addresses the single biggest skepticism an ODR platform faces (will this actually hold up legally?) directly and early

## Weaknesses
- Very thin on formal training/education breadth (one 15-hour course, funnel-only) — not a model for Adjudix's broader Training Institute ambitions
- No visible tiered membership/professional-community layer at all
- Consumer-startup tone, while excellent for approachability, may read as less "institutionally authoritative" for large corporate/cross-border disputes than SIAC/CIArb's gravitas — a positioning trade-off, not a flaw per se
- Panel/neutral profiles and detailed rules content weren't deeply explored in this pass (present in nav as "Panel of Neutrals," "Rules & Procedures" but not opened)

## Elements Worth Reusing (for Adjudix)
- **Embed real product screenshots in device frames** (phone/browser chrome) as the primary proof mechanism for the ODR Platform module — far more convincing than describing features in prose, and directly actionable once Adjudix's own portal has any UI to screenshot
- **Audience-first top-level nav split** (e.g., "For Individuals / For Businesses / For Professionals") as a possible alternate or complementary IA layer alongside the service-first sitemap, especially on the homepage
- **Multi-intent branching contact form** with a query-type dropdown — directly solves Adjudix's own notes' "Query Form" and "Contact Us" items with one well-designed component instead of several separate simple forms
- **A named, brandable arbitration/med-arb clause product** (like "Sama Med-Arb Clause") — turns Adjudix's "Model Clauses" / "Arbitration Clause Drafting" notes into a marketable named asset
- **A distinct "I've been named in this dispute" respondent intake path**, separate from "I want to file a dispute" — essential and easy to miss
- **Proactive enforceability trust content** (citing any future court validation of an Adjudix-administered award, once available) as its own homepage trust block
- **Low-commitment paid-consultation entry point** for individuals unsure if their dispute fits — could map onto Adjudix's own "Query Form" / initial-consult flow before full case filing
- **Include Lok Adalat explicitly** as one of the named mechanisms offered, consistent with Adjudix's own notes and India's statutory ADR landscape
- **Publish redacted award outcomes and panel empanelment norms** as transparency content, supporting Adjudix's "Research & Publications" and panel-governance credibility

## Elements Worth Improving (things Adjudix should do better)
- Balance Sama's consumer-warm tone with enough institutional gravitas for larger commercial/cross-border disputes — Adjudix likely needs a dual register (approachable for individuals/MSMEs, authoritative for corporate/institutional arbitration), whereas Sama leans almost entirely toward the former
- Build out a genuine multi-tier training/certification/membership system (per CIArb's model) alongside Sama-style platform slickness — Sama alone under-invests here relative to Adjudix's stated ambitions
- Where Sama's training is purely a supply-side funnel, Adjudix should also offer training as a standalone value proposition (its own notes list "Training & Certification Programmes" as a first-class service, not just an internal onboarding step)
